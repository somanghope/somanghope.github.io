My research asks how people perceive, feel, and imagine places — and how we can measure those experiences with GeoAI and neuroscience.

Research experience
======

GeoImagine: Reconstructing places from the brain
------
*GISense Lab, UT Austin · Aug 2025 – present*

How does the brain represent the geographic environments we see and imagine? In the GeoImagine project, I am developing **Mind2Place**, a diffusion model that reconstructs geographic scenes from brain signals, and designing fMRI experiments at UT Austin's Biomedical Imaging Center (BIC). A companion study uses AI embeddings to decode neural representations of place from MEG signals.

Human perception of AI-generated vs. authentic imagery
------
*fNIRS study · presented at AAG 2026*

As AI-generated images of places become common, do our brains respond to them the same way as to real photographs? Using functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS), this study explores how people perceive AI-generated versus authentic imagery.

Perceived safety and fear of crime from street view imagery
------
*National Research Foundation of Korea · Jun 2023 – Aug 2025*

How safe does a street *feel*? Using street view images, deep learning (Swin Transformer, Siamese networks), and explainable GeoAI, I measured and mapped perceived fear of crime at the street level and used the models to evaluate CPTED-based (Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design) safety design. Related work assessed walkability from street view imagery.

**Related work**
- **Kim, S.**, Choi, J., & Kang, Y. (2026). Modeling and Explaining Perceived Fear of Crime from Street View Imagery Using a GeoAI Framework. *IJGI*, 15(1), 18. [[DOI]](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijgi15010018)
